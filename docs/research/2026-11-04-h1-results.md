# H1 results: opening-range breakout on stocks in play

Rules pre-registered in `research/harness/h1_orb_in_play.py` before any run.
Universe: long-history symbols, point-in-time liquid (>= Rs 25 cr median turnover), about 25 names.
Costs: full NSE intraday schedule plus slippage per side.

| Split | Slippage | Net R range over 6 configs | t-stat range | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| train 2019-2022 | 5 bps | -0.046 to -0.153 | -2.3 to -3.4 | all lose |
| train 2019-2022 | 0 bps | -0.014 to -0.029 | -0.3 to -1.3 | zero gross edge |
| valid 2023-2024 | 5 bps | -0.017 to -0.057 | -0.5 to -1.4 | all lose |
| holdout 2025-26 | - | not run | - | single-use, not spent |

Gross R before costs is +0.00 to +0.09 on train and up to +0.20 on valid, but costs (about 10-15 bps per round trip
with slippage) exceed it in every config. The contrarian control (trade against the first candle) loses about as
much, so the loss is cost drag, not a mis-specified direction.

Verdict: NO-GO for this form of ORB on this universe. Mean net R is negative in nearly every year.

Caveats that keep this from being the last word on "stocks in play":
1. Universe is thin (about 25 liquid names), so "top 5 by RVOL" selects weak catalysts. The published
   result used thousands of US stocks.
2. Universe is hindsight-picked (many later winners), so it is not a clean sample.
3. Volume-only "in play" signal. No news, earnings or gap-size information.
4. Stop-width and entry rules were the paper's, not tuned. Tuning would not rescue a zero gross edge.
